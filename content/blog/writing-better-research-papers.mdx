After reviewing hundreds of papers and getting many of my own rejected, I have distilled some practical advice for writing better research papers.

## The Golden Rule: Story First

Every great paper tells a story. Before writing a single line, answer these three questions:

1. **What problem are you solving?** Make the reader care.
2. **Why is it hard?** Show the gap in existing work.
3. **What did you do differently?** Your contribution.

## Structure That Works

### Abstract (150-250 words)

Write this last. It should contain: problem, approach, key result, and significance.

### Introduction (1-2 pages)

- Paragraph 1: Set the context, make the reader care
- Paragraph 2-3: Describe the problem and why existing solutions fall short
- Paragraph 4: Your approach and key contributions
- Final paragraph: Paper organization

### Related Work

Don't just list papers. *Compare and contrast*. Show where your work fits in the landscape.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Burying the lead**: State your main result early
- **Passive voice overuse**: "We propose" is clearer than "It is proposed"
- **Missing ablations**: Reviewers will ask "why does each component matter?"
- **Weak baselines**: Compare against the strongest available methods

## The Review Process

Remember: reviewers are busy. Make their job easy:

- Clear figures with descriptive captions
- Tables that are self-contained
- Consistent notation throughout

> A paper that is easy to review is more likely to get accepted, all else being equal.

## Tools I Use

- **LaTeX + Overleaf** for collaborative writing
- **Zotero** for reference management
- **draw.io** for diagrams
- **matplotlib/seaborn** for publication-quality plots

Good writing is a skill that improves with practice. The more you write, review, and revise, the better you get.
